# Code of Conduct — AHA / Ayni

*Ancestral Humanity Anonymous — a worldwide, chain-agnostic fellowship.
**Ayni** is its Solana implementation.*

This is a **fellowship that happens to ship software**, not a startup that
happens to have a community. Our conduct is rooted in the same Traditions that
shape the protocol itself: *group conscience, one member one voice, principles
before personalities, anonymity, and self-support.* This document binds everyone
who participates — contributors, reviewers, Council servants, and members —
wherever AHA gathers: this repository, issues and pull requests, chat, calls,
ceremonies, and Circle meetings.

---

## Our pledge

We are building infrastructure for people to gather, govern themselves, and walk
a shamanic path **without surrendering their identity, their autonomy, or their
voice to capital.** We pledge to make participation in AHA a harassment-free,
coercion-free experience for everyone — regardless of who they are, where they
practice, what tradition they come from, or whether they ever choose to reveal
their name.

We hold ourselves to the standard the protocol enforces in code: **no whales, no
vote-buying, no single point of failure, no one above the group conscience.**

---

## The Traditions, as conduct

The fellowship's design principles are also its social contract. What the code
guarantees, we also practice as people.

| Tradition / value | What it means for how we treat each other |
|---|---|
| **One member, one voice (T2)** | Influence is earned by service and reason, not by tokens, tenure, or volume. No one's view counts for more because they wrote more code or hold a seat. |
| **Principles before personalities (T12)** | Critique ideas, designs, and proofs — never people. Disagree about the *what*, never attack the *who*. |
| **Anonymity is foundational** | Pseudonymity is welcome and protected. Never pressure anyone to reveal their identity, never attempt to de-anonymize a member, and never link a wallet, handle, or metadata trail to a person. Doxxing is the gravest violation here — it attacks the thing the project exists to protect. |
| **Each group autonomous (T4)** | Respect a Circle's right to decide its own affairs. Don't override local group conscience or speak for a Circle you don't belong to. |
| **Fully self-supporting (T7)** | We decline outside money and outside agendas. No undisclosed sponsorship, no steering the protocol toward anyone's profit. Contributions are a gift (*Ayni* — sacred reciprocity), not an investment. |
| **Trusted servants, not officers (T2)** | Maintainers and Council seats serve the fellowship; they do not rule it. Authority is rotating, accountable, and revocable by group conscience. |
| **Unity (T1)** | The common welfare comes first. When personal preference and the fellowship's survival conflict, the fellowship wins. |

## Expected behavior

- **Be welcoming and patient.** Many contributors are practitioners first and
  engineers second, or the reverse. Meet people where they are.
- **Assume good faith** and seek the *group conscience* — the sense of the
  group — rather than winning an argument.
- **Protect anonymity by default.** Treat every identity as private unless its
  holder has clearly chosen to disclose it, and only to the extent they chose.
- **Give and receive feedback gracefully.** Reviews are a gift of attention.
  Thank reviewers; revise without defensiveness.
- **Credit generously, claim modestly.** This is a gift economy.
- **Raise concerns early and through the proper channel** (see *Security* and
  *Reporting* below).

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form, public or private.
- **De-anonymization**: doxxing, attempting to correlate wallets/metadata to
  identities, or pressuring anyone to reveal who they are.
- **Coercion or vote-buying** in any governance process — on-chain or off — or
  any attempt to concentrate voice against *one member, one vote*.
- Introducing **outside financial agendas**: covert sponsorship, steering the
  protocol toward speculation, tradeability, or profit (see Non-goals in
  `PROJECT.md`).
- Sexualized language or imagery; unwelcome attention; personal attacks;
  sustained disruption of meetings, ceremonies, or threads.
- **Knowingly weakening anonymity or recovery guarantees** — e.g. submitting
  code that exfiltrates identity, deploying placeholder verifying keys, or
  bypassing the 4-of-7 / time-lock / contest safeguards — is both a security
  matter and a conduct violation.

## Security & anonymity issues are a conduct matter

Because this project's reason for existing is **provable privacy**, anything that
endangers a member's anonymity or a Circle's resilience is held to the highest
standard of care. If you discover such a flaw:

- **Do not** open a public issue or post a proof-of-concept that could expose a
  real member.
- Report it privately per `SECURITY.md`.
- Never test de-anonymization against real members or live Circles.

Responsible disclosure is honored as an act of service to the fellowship.

---

## Enforcement — by group conscience

Conduct in AHA is upheld the same way governance is: **socially, accountably, and
without a single unaccountable admin.**

1. **Report.** Raise a concern privately to the maintainers / a Council servant
   at the contact in `SECURITY.md` (or, for a local Circle, to its Secretary).
   Reports may be made **anonymously** — that is the whole point.
2. **Listen.** The matter is heard in confidence. The reporter's anonymity is
   protected as rigorously as any member's.
3. **Decide by group conscience.** For anything beyond a clear, immediate harm,
   resolution follows the fellowship's own rule — a **4-of-7 Council vote** at
   the relevant Circle (or the maintainer group for the codebase), not one
   person's decree.
4. **Respond proportionately.** Possible responses range from a private word, to
   removal of a contribution, to suspension of participation, to — for severe or
   repeated violations — removal from the Circle by group conscience.

No servant, maintainer, or Council seat is above this process; the same
time-locked, contestable accountability that governs key recovery governs us.

---

## Scope

This Code applies in all AHA/Ayni spaces and when an individual is representing
the fellowship in public. A separate Circle may adopt stricter local norms by its
own group conscience (T4), but never looser ones than this.
