# Karma

A short, plain description of what karma is in AHA, who can see it, and what
giving it means. Deliberately free of numbers and mechanics: the figures are set
by each Circle's group conscience and can change, so naming them here would only
make this page wrong later. The engineering detail lives in the code and in the
backlog rows for F98 and F100.

---

## What it is

Karma is a small mark of recognition. You gain some when a sponsorship link is
accepted — both the person being sponsored and the person sponsoring them — and
you can be given some by another member who wants to thank you for your help.

That is the whole of it. It is not a level, not a rank you climb toward, and it
unlocks nothing. Nothing in AHA asks how much karma you have before letting you
do something.

## Who can see it

**Everyone.** Your karma is visible to the other members of your Circle, and it
is shown alongside theirs.

This is worth saying plainly rather than letting anyone discover it later. AHA is
built so that members are not ranked against one another, and karma is a
deliberate exception to that — made knowingly, by decision, and recorded as such.
It also cannot be made private after the fact: the number lives on a public
ledger, so it was readable by anyone who cared to look from the moment it
existed. Showing it in the app does not reveal anything that was being kept.

If you would rather not be compared, the honest answer is that karma is not for
you, and nothing in the fellowship requires it of you.

## Giving karma to someone

You can give some of your karma to another member of your Circle to say thank
you. When you do, your total goes down and theirs goes up.

**It costs you nothing in the end.** After a waiting period set by your Circle,
your karma comes back to you — and they keep theirs. The gesture is real for
them and temporary for you. That is the point: thanking someone should not be a
sacrifice, or people will stop doing it.

A few things follow from that, and they are all deliberate:

- **You can only give what you actually have.** There is no allowance to spend
  beyond your own total.
- **There is a limit on any single gesture**, set by your Circle.
- **You can thank each person once.** They can also thank you once, separately —
  those are two different acts. But the same two people cannot keep passing
  karma back and forth, because that would let any pair inflate each other's
  totals indefinitely and the number would stop meaning anything.

So what a member can accumulate this way is bounded by how many different people
have chosen to thank them. That is the only property that makes it worth reading
at all.

## Who decides the numbers

Your Circle's Council does, by the ordinary group-conscience vote — the same
4-of-7 path used for anything else that matters. The size of a sponsorship
credit, the limit on a single gesture, and how long a gift stays out are all
voted, not fixed by whoever wrote the software. A Circle that has never voted on
them runs on a starting set of figures until it does.

If a Circle votes to shorten the waiting period, karma that is already out comes
back sooner. The rule in force is the one in force now, not the one that applied
when the gift was made.

## What karma is not

- It is not a measure of anyone's recovery, service, or worth.
- It does not confer authority. AHA has no leaders, only trusted servants.
- It is not currency, and it cannot be exchanged for anything.
- It is not a record of who helped whom in any detail — only that some
  recognition passed between two members.

Someone with no karma at all is a member in exactly the same standing as
everyone else.
