## Regressions

### 1. CRITICAL (feature-integrity, not Traditions/privacy) — KarmaAward's "once per pair, forever" guard is directional, not pair-symmetric; a role swap between the same two members double-credits karma

- **Epic/feature:** F98 (sponsorship karma), commit `9825320`.
- **What's wrong:** `KarmaAward`'s PDA is seeded
  `["karmaaward", circle, mentee_membership.commitment, wing_membership.commitment]`
  — i.e., by **directed role**, not a canonicalised/order-independent pair.
  `establish_wing_peer` only requires `mentee != wing` and that the signer
  control the mentee side. Nothing stops the same two members from calling it
  a second time with roles reversed (B sponsors A, after A already sponsored
  B). That produces a **second, distinct** `KarmaAward` account —
  `credited = false` on creation — so it pays out again in full.
- **Reproduced live**, on a fresh local validator, with a standalone test
  (written this round, run, and deleted — not committed):
  - A wings B (A is sponsor/wing, B is mentee/sponsee): A→10, B→100 (the
    documented defaults).
  - B wings A (roles swapped, same two commitments, same Circle): a *second*
    `WingPeer` and a *second* `KarmaAward` are created; the credit fires
    again.
  - Result: **A=110, B=110 — a combined total of 220**, against the 110 a
    single directed sponsorship should cap this pair at. Any two colluding
    members can double their combined take with two individually-legitimate
    transactions. No farming loop, no release/re-link — the exact abuse
    pattern the commit's own guard was built to close, just via the one
    direction the guard doesn't check.
- **Why this is not waived by the karma waiver:** the user's waiver accepts
  that karma ranks members; it says nothing about the ranking being gameable.
  The commit's own text claims "once per pair, forever" and states the guard
  "is the point" — that claim is false as shipped. This is reported as a
  functional/integrity regression against the feature's own documented
  guarantee, at CRITICAL severity because it directly undermines the one
  property (anti-farming) the commit exists to provide, and because it was
  explicitly asked to be probed and was not caught by the shipped test suite.
- **Not covered:** `tests/karma.ts`'s farming test only exercises
  release/re-link of the *same* directed pair; no case exercises a role swap.
- **Reproduction (ephemeral test, not committed — reconstructable from this
  description):** stand up a Circle, issue two memberships A and B, call
  `establishWingPeer(mentee=B, wing=A)`, then `establishWingPeer(mentee=A,
  wing=B)`; fetch both `Karma` accounts; combined total is 220, not ≤110.
- **First bad commit:** `9825320` (introduced with the feature; no prior
  version to bisect against — this is new code).
- **Suggested remediation (not performed — Sentinel does not fix code):**
  canonicalise the `KarmaAward` seed (e.g. a lexicographically sorted
  commitment pair) so both directions collide on one account, or add an
  explicit `require!` that the reverse-direction `WingPeer`/`KarmaAward` does
  not already exist, plus a regression test asserting the pair's *combined*
  total after a role swap.

## Verdict rationale

Karma is allowed to rank people now — the user said so plainly, and this
report does not argue with that. What this round checked instead is whether
the fence built around that decision holds, and whether the fellowship's own
words were left alone. The fence around the *wording* of the Twelve
Traditions holds: only the three sentences that were asked to change,
changed, in all nineteen languages, and Tradition 2 itself — the rule that
was waived — was not touched, which is exactly what was promised. The fence
around karma's forbidden-word list also holds: planting fake "score" and
"rating" fields into the codebase and running the gate showed it still
catches them, even with "karma" now let through on its own. But the fence
meant to stop two members from gaming their own ranking does not hold in the
one direction nobody tested: if two people sponsor each other, once each, in
either order, they walk away with double the points a single sponsorship
should have paid — confirmed by actually running it on a test blockchain, not
just by reading the code. That is reported as the round's one serious finding,
alongside a smaller paperwork problem (the project's own "what's shipped"
documents were not updated to say karma shipped, or that it's waiting no
longer). Nothing here touches money, names, or private relationships — it is
about the ranking itself being fair on its own terms, which is what remains of
Tradition 2's promise once the no-ranking rule was set aside.
